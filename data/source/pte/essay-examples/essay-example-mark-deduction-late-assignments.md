## The Essay

In recent years, the topic of mark deduction for late assignments in universities has emerged as a prominent subject of public and academic discussion. I contend that deducting students' marks can be an effective approach to encourage timely submission, though supportive measures should also be implemented, and the following analysis presents the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of mark deduction is its potential to facilitate academic discipline and time management skills. In particular, implementing deadline policies for assignments can foster student responsibility and respect for academic standards, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as improved work habits and better preparation for professional responsibilities. For instance, educational research reveals that approximately 80% of students with clear deadline policies experienced enhanced submission punctuality as a direct result of mark deduction systems.

However, it is equally important to acknowledge that other recommendations may also give rise to more effective solutions. This is largely because supportive measures such as deadline extensions for valid reasons and student support systems are likely to generate positive motivation rather than penalty-based approaches, which may subsequently cause students to develop better planning skills voluntarily. According to research by educational institutions, over 60% of universities achieved improved submission rates through flexible policies stemming from comprehensive student support services.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that while mark deduction has considerable merit as a disciplinary approach, combining it with supportive recommendations creates balanced outcomes for universities and students, as the influence of both accountability and flexibility is substantial and far-reaching. Thus, it is imperative for educational institutions to dedicate adequate resources and planning to managing this issue effectively.
