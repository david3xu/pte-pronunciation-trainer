## Topic
> It is argued that getting married before finishing school or getting a job is foolish. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

## The Essay

In recent years, the topic of getting married before finishing school or getting a job has emerged as a prominent subject of public and academic discussion. I contend that early marriage presents significant challenges for young people, particularly regarding education and career development, and the following analysis presents the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one key challenge of early marriage is its potential to facilitate limited opportunities. In particular, getting married before finishing school can foster restricted growth in education and career paths, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as reduced income potential and fewer professional choices. For instance, evidence from social research reveals that approximately 80% of young people who married early experienced personal development challenges as a direct result of early commitment to family responsibilities.

However, it is equally important to acknowledge that married couples may also give rise to certain benefits in specific contexts. This is largely because committed relationships are likely to generate emotional support and family stability, which may subsequently cause young couples to build stronger foundations. According to family research, over 60% of married couples have been able to achieve partnership benefits resulting from mutual support systems.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that getting married before finishing school or getting a job is generally challenging, as the influence of limited opportunities and restricted personal growth is substantial and meaningful. Thus, it is essential for young people to dedicate adequate time and consideration to managing this life decision effectively.
