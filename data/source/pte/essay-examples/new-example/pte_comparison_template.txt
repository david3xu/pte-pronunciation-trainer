## 🎯 When to Use COMPARISON Template

### **Use COMPARISON when question asks:**

✅ **"Which do you prefer?"**
- Example: "Countryside vs City - which do you prefer?"

✅ **"Which is better/more important?"**
- Example: "X or Y - which is more important?"

✅ **"Who should take responsibility?"**
- Example: "Governments, companies, or individuals - who should?"

✅ **"What is most important/best?"**
- Example: "What is the most important invention?"

## 📝 COMPARISON Template Structure

### **Word Budget:**
- Introduction: 50 words
- Para 2 (Why X is better): 85 words
- Para 3 (Y has value BUT X still better): 70 words
- Conclusion: 30 words
- **Total: ~235 words**

---

### **INTRODUCTION (50 words)**

```
The question of [comparison topic] has attracted considerable attention 
in modern society. When comparing [Option X] and [Option Y], I contend 
that [Option X] is [more important/better/preferable] for [subject] 
despite [acknowledgment of Y], and the following analysis outlines the 
primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.
```

**Key Points:**
- Clearly state BOTH options being compared
- State your preference immediately
- Acknowledge the alternative exists

---

### **PARAGRAPH 2 - WHY X IS BETTER (85 words)**

```
To begin with, one compelling advantage of [Option X] is its potential 
to bring about [outcome 1]. In particular, [Option X] can facilitate 
[outcome 2], ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as [outcome 3]. 
Additionally, [Option X] provides [outcome 4] that [Option Y] cannot 
match.

[Subject group] who [choose X] demonstrate [results]. For instance, 
[specific subjects] who [use X] report significantly [evidence result] 
compared to those who [use Y].
```

**Key Points:**
- Give 3-4 strong advantages of your choice
- Show concrete benefits
- Include comparison with alternative
- Use ONE idiom naturally here

---

### **PARAGRAPH 3 - Y HAS VALUE BUT X BETTER (70 words)**

```
However, it is important to acknowledge that [Option Y] also provides 
certain benefits. This is largely because [Option Y] can offer [benefit], 
which may enable [secondary outcome]. 

Nevertheless, [Option X] remains superior because [key reason why X 
is fundamentally better]. As a result, [specific context] shows that 
[Option X] delivers [superior outcome] that make it the better choice 
overall.
```

**Key Points:**
- Acknowledge alternative fairly
- Show you understand its merits
- BUT reinforce why your choice is still better
- Use transition: "Nevertheless", "However", "Despite this"

---

### **CONCLUSION (30 words)**

```
Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that [Option X] is 
preferable to [Option Y], since its advantages are more significant. 
Thus, it is essential to prioritize [Option X] effectively.
```

**Key Points:**
- Reaffirm your preference
- Brief and clear
- No new information

## 🔄 Key Differences from PRIMARY Template

| Aspect | PRIMARY Template | COMPARISON Template |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Question Type** | Agree/Disagree, Opinion, Adv/Disadv | Preference, Which is better |
| **Introduction** | State position on one topic | State preference between TWO options |
| **Para 2** | Benefits of the topic | Why Option X is BETTER than Y |
| **Para 3** | Challenges of topic | Y has merit BUT X still superior |
| **Conclusion** | Reaffirm importance | Reaffirm X > Y |
| **Key Word** | "despite limitations" | "despite [Y's value], X is better" |

---

## 📊 Word Count Guidelines

| Section | Target Words | What to Include |
|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Introduction** | 50 words | Both options + clear preference |
| **Paragraph 2** | 85 words | 3-4 advantages of your choice + evidence |
| **Paragraph 3** | 70 words | Alternative's value + why yours is still better |
| **Conclusion** | 30 words | Reaffirm preference |
| **TOTAL** | **~235 words** | Optimal range |

---

## ❌ Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why Wrong | Fix |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| **Not stating preference in intro** | Unclear position | "I contend that X is better than Y" |
| **Giving equal weight to both** | Sounds indecisive | X gets 85 words, Y gets 70 words |
| **Forgetting to compare** | Sounds like PRIMARY template | Always include "compared to Y" |
| **Changing position** | Inconsistent | Maintain X > Y throughout |
| **Not acknowledging Y** | Unfair evaluation | Para 3 must show Y's merits |

## Example 1: Countryside vs City Life

### **Question:**
> "Living in the countryside or having a city life, which one do you prefer? Please use examples or your personal experience to support your opinion."

### **Question Type:** Preference (Which do you prefer?)

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 32):**

**Option X (Preference):** living in the countryside  
**Option Y (Alternative):** city life

**Subjects:** people, individuals

**Why Countryside (X) is Better:**
- clean air and natural environment
- peaceful lifestyle and less stress
- stronger community and closer neighbors
- lower costs and affordable housing

**Why City Has Value (Y):**
- job opportunities and career growth
- better facilities and public transport
- entertainment options and social activities

**Idiom:** "hustle and bustle" (busy city life)

**Stakeholders:** individuals and families

---

### **Complete Essay (236 words)**

The question of **whether countryside or city living is preferable** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. When comparing **living in the countryside** and **city life**, I contend that **countryside living** is **more beneficial** for **people** despite **the advantages cities offer**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of **living in the countryside** is its potential to bring about **clean air and natural environment**. In particular, **countryside living** can facilitate **peaceful lifestyle and reduced stress**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **stronger community connections and closer neighbor relationships**. Additionally, **countryside living** provides **lower costs and affordable housing** that **city life** cannot match. **Residents in rural areas** demonstrate **better mental health and life satisfaction**. For instance, **families who choose countryside homes** report significantly **improved wellbeing and stronger community bonds** compared to those **in urban environments**.

However, it is important to acknowledge that **city life** also provides certain benefits. This is largely because **cities** can offer **job opportunities and career growth**, which may enable **access to better facilities and diverse entertainment**. Nevertheless, **countryside living** remains superior because **quality of life and mental health** outweigh career convenience. As a result, **people seeking balanced lifestyles** show that **rural living** away from **the hustle and bustle** delivers **better overall satisfaction** that make it the better choice overall.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that **countryside living** is preferable to **city life**, since its advantages are more significant. Thus, it is essential to prioritize **rural living** for long-term wellbeing.

## Example 2: Public Transport vs Roads

### **Question:**
> "As cities are expanding, some people claim governments should look forward to creating better networks of public transport available for everyone rather than building more roads for vehicle owning population. What's your opinion?"

### **Question Type:** Which is better (for government investment)

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 34):**

**Option X (Preference):** public transport networks  
**Option Y (Alternative):** building more roads

**Subjects:** governments, citizens

**Why Public Transport (X) is Better:**
- reduced traffic and lower pollution
- affordable travel and equal access
- environmental benefits and cleaner air
- less traffic congestion

**Why Roads Have Value (Y):**
- personal convenience and travel flexibility
- economic growth and business transport
- infrastructure development

**Idiom:** "cutting-edge" (modern, advanced)

**Stakeholders:** governments and city planners

---

### **Complete Essay (238 words)**

The question of **whether governments should invest in public transport or road expansion** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. When comparing **public transport networks** and **building more roads**, I contend that **public transport** is **more important** for **cities** despite **the value of road infrastructure**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of **public transport networks** is their potential to bring about **reduced traffic congestion and lower pollution**. In particular, **public transport** can facilitate **affordable travel and equal access for all citizens**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **environmental benefits and cleaner air quality**. Additionally, **public transport** provides **cutting-edge solutions to urban crowding** that **more roads** cannot achieve. **Cities with strong public transport** demonstrate **better air quality and mobility**. For instance, **urban areas that invest in trains and buses** report significantly **reduced car dependency and pollution** compared to those **focused on road expansion**.

However, it is important to acknowledge that **building more roads** also provides certain benefits. This is largely because **road infrastructure** can offer **personal convenience and economic growth**, which may enable **business transport and flexibility**. Nevertheless, **public transport** remains superior because **sustainability and equality** outweigh individual convenience. As a result, **forward-thinking cities** show that **public transport investment** delivers **better long-term urban development** that make it the better choice overall.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that **public transport networks** are preferable to **road expansion**, since their advantages are more significant. Thus, it is essential to prioritize **public transport development** effectively.

- public funding and policy coordination

- company efforts and private actions
- business innovation and individual changes

### **Complete Essay (235 words)**

The question of **who should bear primary responsibility for addressing climate change** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. When comparing **governments**, **companies**, and **individuals**, I contend that **governments should take primary responsibility** for **climate action** despite **the valuable roles of other groups**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of **government-led climate action** is its potential to bring about **comprehensive environmental laws and national regulations**. In particular, **governments** can facilitate **public funding and coordinated policy implementation**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **strong enforcement and ambitious climate targets**. Additionally, **governments** possess **authority for international cooperation** that **companies and individuals** cannot match. **Nations with government climate leadership** demonstrate **significant emission reductions**. For instance, **countries where governments take the lead on climate policy** report significantly **stronger environmental outcomes** compared to those **relying on voluntary corporate or individual action**.

However, it is important to acknowledge that **companies and individuals** also provide certain contributions. This is largely because **business innovation and personal lifestyle changes** can offer **complementary climate benefits**, which may enable **broader public engagement**. Nevertheless, **government action** remains superior because **only governments have regulatory power** to enforce necessary changes. As a result, **successful climate programs** show that **government leadership** delivers **systemic change** that make it the better approach overall.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that **governments** are the most appropriate primary actors, since their capabilities are most significant. Thus, it is essential to prioritize **government-led climate action** effectively.

## Example 4: Most Important Invention

### **Question:**
> "There are many important inventions in the modern world, including antibiotics, airplanes and computers. What do you believe is the most important invention of the past hundred years?"

**Why Internet (X) is Most Important:**
- global communication and worldwide connections
- instant information access and knowledge sharing
- online learning and business growth
- transforms all aspects of life

**Why Others Have Value (Y):**
- antibiotics save lives
- airplanes enable travel
- each has specific benefits

**Idiom:** "game-changer" (revolutionary innovation)

**Stakeholders:** society and individuals

---

### **Complete Essay (234 words)**

The question of **which invention has been most important in the past century** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. When comparing **the internet**, **antibiotics**, **airplanes**, and **other innovations**, I contend that **the internet** is **the most significant** for **society** despite **the importance of other inventions**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of **the internet** is its potential to bring about **global communication and instant worldwide connections**. In particular, **the internet** can facilitate **immediate information access and knowledge sharing**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **online learning, business growth, and social transformation**. Additionally, **the internet** serves as **a game-changer affecting every aspect of modern life** that **other inventions** cannot match. **Societies with internet access** demonstrate **unprecedented development and innovation**. For instance, **people with internet connectivity** report significantly **better educational opportunities and economic advancement** compared to those **without digital access**.

However, it is important to acknowledge that **other inventions like antibiotics and airplanes** also provide certain crucial benefits. This is largely because **medical advances save lives** and **transportation innovations connect people**, which may enable **specific important outcomes**. Nevertheless, **the internet** remains superior because **it amplifies and enables all other innovations** while transforming society fundamentally. As a result, **modern civilization** shows that **internet connectivity** delivers **the most comprehensive impact** that make it the most important invention overall.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that **the internet** is the most important invention, since its impact is most significant. Thus, it is essential to recognize **the internet's transformative role** in modern society.

## ✅ Checklist for COMPARISON Essays

Before submitting:

**Content:**
- [ ] Both options clearly stated in intro
- [ ] Clear preference stated immediately
- [ ] 3-4 advantages of preferred option
- [ ] Alternative acknowledged fairly in Para 3
- [ ] "Nevertheless" or "However" showing X still better
- [ ] Consistent preference throughout

**Structure:**
- [ ] Word count: 230-240 words
- [ ] Para 2 longer than Para 3 (85w vs 70w)
- [ ] ONE idiom placed naturally
- [ ] Comparison included ("compared to those who...")
- [ ] Conclusion reaffirms preference

**Language:**
- [ ] "When comparing X and Y..."
- [ ] "X is preferable/better/more important than Y"
- [ ] "Nevertheless, X remains superior because..."
- [ ] B1 vocabulary throughout

## 🎯 Key Success Factors

**What Makes COMPARISON Different:**

1. ✅ **State TWO options explicitly** (not just one topic)
2. ✅ **Make your preference crystal clear** from introduction
3. ✅ **Show you understand both sides** (fair evaluation)
4. ✅ **BUT maintain consistent position** (don't waver)
5. ✅ **Use "Nevertheless" to pivot** back to your preference

**Common Comparison Words:**
- preferable to, superior to, better than
- more important than, more effective than
- remains the better choice, delivers superior outcomes
- cannot match, outweighs
