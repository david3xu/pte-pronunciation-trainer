## Example 13: New Invention Impact (Smartphones)

### **Question:**
> "In our technological world, the number of new inventions has been evolving on a daily basis. Please describe a new invention and explain the impact, either beneficial or detrimental, that you feel it has had on society."

### **Question Type:** Advantages and Disadvantages (describe and explain impact)

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 13):**

**Main topic:** smartphones  
**Paraphrases:** mobile devices, these devices, smartphone technology, modern phones

**Subjects:** people, users

**Positive outcomes:** instant communication and global connectivity, easy access to information and online services, online learning and improved productivity

**Negative outcomes:** screen addiction and excessive usage, less face-to-face contact and social isolation, sleep problems and mental health concerns

**Idiom:** "stay connected" (maintaining communication)

**Stakeholders:** individuals and technology companies

---

### **Complete Essay (237 words)**

The question of **how modern mobile devices have impacted society** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. I contend that **smartphones offer substantial benefits** for **people** despite **certain limitations**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of **smartphones** is their potential to bring about **instant communication and global connectivity**. In particular, **mobile devices** can facilitate **easy access to information and online services**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **online learning and improved productivity**. **People who use smartphones effectively** demonstrate **better time management and information access**. For instance, **users who utilize smartphone apps for work and education** report significantly **improved efficiency and learning opportunities** compared to those **without smartphone access**.

However, it is equally important to acknowledge that **smartphone technology** faces certain challenges in **modern society**. This is largely because **heavy smartphone use** creates **screen addiction and excessive device checking**, which then lead to **reduced face-to-face interaction and social isolation**. **Many users** feel pressure to **stay connected through their devices constantly**, which creates **sleep problems and declining mental health**. As a result, many **people** have **difficulty maintaining healthy relationships** that make it difficult to **balance digital and real-world interactions**.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate the overall importance of **smartphones**, since their advantages remain significant despite the issues that accompany them. Thus, it is essential for **individuals and technology companies** to provide sufficient support to address these challenges effectively.

## Example 14: Maximum Wage Limits

### **Question:**
> "Should there be a maximum wage for high-paying people? To what extent do you agree with this statement?"

### **Question Type:** Agree/Disagree

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 17):**

**Main topic:** maximum wage limits  
**Paraphrases:** wage caps, salary limits, income restrictions, maximum earnings

**Subjects:** high earners, society

**Positive outcomes:** social fairness and equal opportunities, reduced poverty and better public services, community spirit and social responsibility

**Negative outcomes:** reduced motivation and talent loss, economic problems and lower productivity, recruitment difficulties and higher staff turnover

**Idiom:** "level the playing field" (creating fairness)

**Stakeholders:** governments and policymakers

---

### **Complete Essay (234 words)**

The question of **whether governments should impose salary limits on high earners** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. I contend that **maximum wage limits offer substantial benefits** for **society** despite **certain limitations**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of **maximum wage limits** is their potential to bring about **social fairness and equal opportunities**. In particular, **wage caps** can facilitate **reduced poverty and better public services**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **stronger community spirit and social responsibility**. **Societies with income restrictions** demonstrate **more equitable resource distribution and reduced inequality**. For instance, **countries that implement progressive wage policies** report significantly **improved social cohesion and public satisfaction** compared to those **with unlimited executive compensation**.

However, it is equally important to acknowledge that **salary limits** face certain challenges in **competitive economies**. This is largely because **income restrictions** create **reduced motivation for high achievers**, which then lead to **talent loss and economic inefficiency**. **Many organizations** that cannot **offer competitive salaries struggle to level the playing field for top talent**, which creates **recruitment difficulties and decreased innovation**. As a result, many **companies** have **difficulty attracting skilled professionals** that make it difficult to **maintain competitive advantages**.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate the overall importance of **maximum wage limits**, since their advantages remain significant despite the issues that accompany them. Thus, it is essential for **governments and policymakers** to provide sufficient support to address these challenges effectively.

## Example 15: Modern Childhood

### **Question:**
> "It is getting harder for children to live and grow in the 21st century than in the past. Do you agree or disagree?"

### **Question Type:** Agree/Disagree

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 18):**

**Main topic:** modern childhood  
**Paraphrases:** childhood today, growing up in modern times, contemporary childhood, children's lives today

**Subjects:** children, parents

**Positive outcomes:** better medical treatment and improved nutrition, easier access to education and technological advances, higher living standards and safer environments

**Negative outcomes:** academic pressure and excessive screen time, social isolation and mental pressure, academic stress and emotional difficulties

**Idiom:** "screen time" (time spent on devices - this counts as colloquialism)

**Stakeholders:** parents and schools

---

### **Complete Essay (232 words)**

The question of **whether growing up in modern times is more difficult than in the past** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. I disagree that **childhood today** is harder, contending that **modern childhood offers substantial benefits** for **children** despite **certain limitations**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of **modern childhood** is its potential to bring about **better medical treatment and improved nutrition**. In particular, **childhood today** can facilitate **easier access to education and technological resources**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **higher living standards and safer environments**. **Children in modern societies** demonstrate **better health and educational outcomes**. For instance, **young people with access to modern healthcare and education** report significantly **improved physical health and learning opportunities** compared to those **in previous generations**.

However, it is equally important to acknowledge that **contemporary childhood** faces certain challenges in **modern society**. This is largely because **current educational systems** create **academic pressure and high expectations**, which then lead to **mental stress and emotional difficulties**. **Many children** experience pressure from **excessive screen time and digital distractions**, which creates **social isolation and reduced outdoor activity**. As a result, many **young people** have **difficulty managing stress effectively** that make it difficult to **maintain healthy development**.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that **modern childhood** has more advantages than past eras, since benefits remain significant despite new challenges. Thus, it is essential for **parents and schools** to provide sufficient support to address these challenges effectively.

## Example 16: Mass Media Influence

### **Question:**
> "The mass media, including TV, radio and newspapers, influences our society and shapes our opinions and characters. What is your opinion?"

### **Question Type:** Opinion

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 26):**

**Main topic:** mass media influence  
**Paraphrases:** media impact, media's role, influence of media, media effects

**Subjects:** individuals, society

**Positive outcomes:** public awareness and informed citizens, educational programs and knowledge sharing, cultural understanding and global perspectives

**Negative outcomes:** misinformation and biased reporting, excessive screen time and passive consumption, reduced critical thinking and blind trust

**Idiom:** "take with a grain of salt" (be skeptical) - BUT this is complex, so use simpler: "around the clock" for news availability

**Stakeholders:** media organizations and society

---

### **Complete Essay (236 words)**

The question of **whether media significantly shapes public opinion and character** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. I contend that **mass media influence offers substantial benefits** for **society** despite **certain limitations**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of **mass media influence** is its potential to bring about **public awareness and informed citizenship**. In particular, **media impact** can facilitate **educational programs and knowledge sharing**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **cultural understanding and global perspectives**. **Individuals who engage with quality media** demonstrate **better understanding of current events and social issues**. For instance, **people who regularly consume news from multiple sources** report significantly **improved civic awareness and critical thinking** compared to those **without regular media access**.

However, it is equally important to acknowledge that **media's role** faces certain challenges in **information-rich societies**. This is largely because **unreliable media sources** create **misinformation and biased reporting**, which then lead to **reduced critical thinking and uncritical acceptance**. **Many people** receive **news around the clock from various platforms**, which creates **information overload and difficulty distinguishing reliable sources**. As a result, many **individuals** have **difficulty forming independent opinions** that make it difficult to **navigate media messages effectively**.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate the overall importance of **mass media influence**, since its advantages remain significant despite the issues that accompany it. Thus, it is essential for **media organizations and society** to provide sufficient support to address these challenges effectively.

## Example 17: Working Less Hours

### **Question:**
> "In the future, people will work less hours at their jobs. To what extent do you agree with it?"

### **Question Type:** Opinion/Future Prediction (Do you agree this will happen?)

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 21):**

**Main topic:** working fewer hours  
**Paraphrases:** reduced work time, shorter working hours, less time at work, decreased work hours

**Subjects:** employees, workers

**Positive outcomes (Why it will happen):** technological advances and digital automation, artificial intelligence and improved efficiency, higher productivity and greater flexibility

**Negative outcomes (Why it might not):** economic pressure and business competition, employer expectations and workload demands, financial needs and career requirements

**Idiom:** "down the road" (in the future)

**Stakeholders:** employers and governments

---

### **Complete Essay (235 words)**

The question of **whether people will spend less time at work in the future** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. I contend that **working fewer hours is likely** for **employees** despite **certain challenges**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling reason why **reduced work time** will occur is its connection to **technological advances and digital automation**. In particular, **shorter working hours** can result from **artificial intelligence and improved efficiency**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **higher productivity despite less time at work**. **Workers in automated industries** demonstrate **better output with reduced hours**. For instance, **employees in companies that adopt automation** report significantly **improved work-life balance and maintained productivity** compared to those **in traditional manual-labor industries**.

However, it is equally important to acknowledge that **decreased work hours** face certain challenges in **competitive economies**. This is largely because **global business competition** creates **economic pressure and high performance expectations**, which then lead to **employer demands for longer availability and increased workloads**. **Many businesses** believe **employees working fewer hours down the road will reduce competitiveness**, which creates **resistance to implementing shorter schedules**. As a result, many **workers** have **difficulty reducing their hours in practice** that make it difficult to **achieve this future vision universally**.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that **working fewer hours** is probable in the future, since technological trends support this direction despite economic resistance. Thus, it is essential for **employers and governments** to provide sufficient support to address these challenges effectively.

## Example 18: Tourism in Developing Countries

### **Question:**
> "For a less developed country, the disadvantages of tourism are as great as the advantages. Please discuss this statement, and give and explain your opinion."

### **Question Type:** Opinion (Agree/Disagree with evaluation)

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 24):**

**Main topic:** tourism in developing countries  
**Paraphrases:** tourism development, tourism industry, tourism growth, international tourism

**Subjects:** local communities, citizens

**Positive outcomes:** tourism income and economic benefits, local pride and community spirit, job opportunities and business growth

**Negative outcomes:** environmental damage and resource depletion, cultural erosion and local traditions being lost, financial pressure and rising prices

**Idiom:** "pros and cons" (advantages and disadvantages)

**Stakeholders:** governments and local communities

---

### **Complete Essay (238 words)**

The question of **whether tourism development brings equal benefits and drawbacks to less developed nations** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. I contend that **tourism in developing countries offers substantial benefits** that outweigh disadvantages for **local communities** despite **certain limitations**, and the following analysis outlines the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.

To begin with, one compelling advantage of **tourism in developing countries** is its potential to bring about **significant tourism income and economic benefits**. In particular, **tourism development** can facilitate **job opportunities and business growth**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **local pride and improved community infrastructure**. **Communities that embrace tourism** demonstrate **better economic conditions and employment rates**. For instance, **regions that develop tourism industries** report significantly **higher local incomes and better living standards** compared to those **without tourism development**.

However, it is equally important to acknowledge that **tourism growth** faces certain challenges in **developing regions**. This is largely because **rapid tourism expansion** creates **environmental damage and resource depletion**, which then lead to **cultural erosion and loss of traditional practices**. **Many local communities** must **weigh the pros and cons of tourism development carefully**, which creates **rising living costs and pressure on local resources**. As a result, many **citizens** have **difficulty maintaining traditional lifestyles** that make it difficult to **preserve cultural identity**.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that **tourism in developing countries** has greater advantages than disadvantages, since economic benefits remain significant despite cultural and environmental concerns. Thus, it is essential for **governments and local communities** to provide sufficient support to address these challenges effectively.
