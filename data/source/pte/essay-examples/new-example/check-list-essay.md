## ✅ THE COMPLETE CHECKLIST

### **PHASE 1: QUESTION ANALYSIS & VOCABULARY SOURCE**

**Before writing, analyze the question:**

- [ ] **1.1 Question Type Identified**
  - Is it Agree/Disagree, Opinion, Adv/Disadv, How Important/Why, or Comparison?
  - Does it ask for **recommendations or solutions**?
  - Does it ask **two separate parts** (like "How important? Why hard?")?

- [ ] **1.2 Correct Template Selected**
  - PRIMARY template → Most questions (opinion, agree/disagree, adv/disadv)
  - COMPARISON template → "Which is better? Who should?"
  - PROBLEM-SOLUTION template → "Give solutions" as main focus
  - PRIMARY with recommendations → "Opinion AND recommendations" (keep PRIMARY, add recommendations in Para 3)

- [ ] **1.3 Question Requirements Clear**
  - What exactly does question ask for?
  - Opinion only? Or opinion + recommendations?
  - One part or two parts?

- [ ] **1.4 B1 Example Found** 🔴 **CRITICAL - DON'T SKIP!**
  - Open **essay-examples-template-b1.md** document
  - Find the matching B1 example for your question
  - This is your vocabulary source!

- [ ] **1.5 Fill-in Terms Extracted** 🔴 **CRITICAL**

  From the B1 example, copy these terms:

  ```
  Main topic: [what the essay is about]
  Paraphrases: [3-4 variations of the main topic]
  Subjects: [who is affected - people, students, workers, etc.]
  Positive outcomes: [4-6 benefit terms]
  Negative outcomes: [4-6 challenge terms]
  Idiom: [ONE natural idiom for Para 3]
  Stakeholders: [who should take action]
  Evidence sources: [who demonstrates results]
  ```

  **Example from Work-Life Balance (B1 Example 12):**
  ```
  Main topic: work-life balance
  Paraphrases: maintaining this equilibrium, achieving this balance,
               a balanced lifestyle
  Subjects: people, employees, workers
  Positive outcomes: improved mental health and emotional stability,
                     family time and leisure activities,
                     personal happiness and reduced stress
  Negative outcomes: high career expectations and competitive pressure,
                     long working hours and excessive job demands,
                     time constraints and competing priorities
  Idiom: "around the clock"
  Stakeholders: employers and individuals
  ```

**⚠️ WHY THIS IS CRITICAL:**
- B1 vocabulary is already pre-selected at the right level
- No need to guess if words are C1 or B1-B2
- All terms are proven to work for 90+ scoring
- Saves time and prevents vocabulary errors

**❌ Common Mistake:** Writing without extracting B1 terms first, then having to fix vocabulary later

### **PHASE 2: VOCABULARY LEVEL (CRITICAL!)**

**Check EVERY word - this is where most errors happen:**

#### **2.1 Remove ALL C1 Words**

Common C1 words to avoid:

| ❌ C1 Word | ✅ B1-B2 Replacement |
|-----------|---------------------|
| intensive | frequent, demanding, heavy |
| optimal | strong, good, best |
| passively | inactive, without action |
| procrastination | (just remove or use "delay") |
| rigid | strict, firm |
| subsequently | then, later, after that |
| regardless of | no matter what, despite |
| accountability | responsibility, fairness |
| facilitate | help, enable, support |
| mitigate | reduce, lessen |
| exacerbate | worsen, increase |
| ameliorate | improve, better |

- [ ] **2.2 Scanned entire essay for C1 words**
- [ ] **2.3 All words are B1-B2 level**
- [ ] **2.4 No overly simple words** (keep "implement", "facilitate" - these are fine B2)

**How to check:** If unsure about a word, use simpler/more common alternative.

---

### **PHASE 3: SENTENCE STRUCTURE & AWKWARDNESS**

**Check that sentences flow naturally:**

- [ ] **3.1 No awkward word order**
  - ❌ "emphasize exam performance heavily"
  - ✅ "heavily emphasize exam performance"

- [ ] **3.2 No overly complex sentences**
  - Keep sentences clear and readable
  - One main idea per sentence

- [ ] **3.3 No unclear references**
  - Clear who/what each pronoun refers to
  - No ambiguous "this" or "it"

**❌ Common Mistake:** Creating sentences that sound like translations or robot-written

---

### **PHASE 4: TEMPLATE ADHERENCE (MOST CRITICAL!)**

**This is where Examples 2-7 all had the SAME error:**

#### **4.1 Introduction Structure** ✅

```
The question of [topic restatement] has attracted considerable attention
in modern society. I contend that [topic paraphrase] is/offers [position]
for [subject] despite [acknowledgment], and the following analysis outlines
the primary arguments supporting this viewpoint.
```

- [ ] All template phrases present
- [ ] Position clearly stated
- [ ] ~45 words

---

#### **4.2 Para 2 Structure** ✅

```
To begin with, one compelling advantage/reason why [topic] is [evaluation]
lies in its potential/impact on [outcome 1]. In particular, [topic paraphrase]
can facilitate [outcome 2], ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as
[outcome 3].

[Subject group] who [specific action] demonstrate [results]. For instance,
[specific subjects] with/who [condition] report significantly [evidence]
compared to those [without/who don't].
```

- [ ] "To begin with..." phrase present
- [ ] "In particular... can facilitate... ultimately leading to..." structure present
- [ ] Evidence with comparison included
- [ ] ~75-85 words

---

#### **4.3 Para 3 Sentence Structure (CRITICAL!)** ⚠️

**This is where 5/6 examples had errors!**

**Template for Para 3 sentences 1-3:**

```
Sentence 1: However, it is important to acknowledge that [topic paraphrase]
            faces/presents certain challenges in [context].

Sentence 2: This is largely because [root cause] can generate [intermediate
            effect], which may subsequently/then lead to [direct obstacle].

Sentence 3: [Specific context] that [behavior] create/put pressure
            on/for [subject] to [concrete action].
```

**CRITICAL CHECKS for Sentence 3:**

- [ ] **4.3.1 Clear WHO experiences the pressure**
  - NOT: "Students create pressure to..." ❌
  - YES: "Students experience pressure to..." ✅
  - OR: "Workplaces create pressure for employees to..." ✅

- [ ] **4.3.2 Concrete ACTION stated** (not abstract!)
  - NOT: "cope with schedules" ❌ (too abstract)
  - YES: "memorize facts rather than understand concepts" ✅
  - YES: "respond to emails after hours" ✅
  - YES: "sacrifice sleep and personal time" ✅

- [ ] **4.3.3 Logical cause → effect chain**
  - Does the chain make sense?
  - Is it: Context → Creates pressure → Subject responds?
  - NOT backwards (subject creating pressure on themselves)

- [ ] **4.3.4 Natural English phrasing**
  - Does it sound like natural academic writing?
  - Not forced or awkward?

**❌ WRONG PATTERNS TO AVOID:**
```
"Students who X create pressure to Y"
"Schools that X create pressure to balance Y"
"People who X create pressure to spend time Y"
```

**✅ CORRECT PATTERNS:**
```
"Situations/Contexts that X create pressure for [subjects] to Y"
"[Subjects] who X experience pressure to Y"
"[Subjects] find themselves Y-ing"
```

---

#### **4.4 Idiom Placement**

- [ ] **4.4.1 ONE idiom used in Para 3**
- [ ] **4.4.2 Placed naturally** (usually sentence 3)
- [ ] **4.4.3 Appropriate level** (B1-B2, not too casual)
- [ ] **4.4.4 Fits context** naturally

**Good idioms:**
- around-the-clock (constant availability)
- wind down (relax)
- juggle (manage multiple things)
- make the grade (meet standards)
- hands-on (direct experience)

---

#### **4.5 Conclusion Structure**

**For standard essays:**
```
Taken together, these considerations demonstrate [summary]. Thus, it is
essential for [stakeholders] to [action] effectively.
```

- [ ] Brief summary of position
- [ ] ~35-40 words
- [ ] No new information

**For essays with recommendations:**
- [ ] References the specific recommendations given in Para 3
- [ ] Still stays brief (~35-40 words)

---

### **PHASE 5: LOGIC CHAIN VERIFICATION**

**Verify the cause → effect logic makes sense:**

#### **5.1 Para 2 Logic Chain**

```
CLAIM: Topic → Outcome 1 → Outcome 2 → Outcome 3
EVIDENCE: Subject demonstrates results that support these outcomes
```

- [ ] Each arrow is logical cause → effect
- [ ] Evidence supports the claim
- [ ] No backwards causality

#### **5.2 Para 3 Logic Chain**

```
CLAIM: Root cause → Intermediate effect → Direct obstacle
EVIDENCE: Specific context shows this pattern
```

- [ ] Root cause actually creates the intermediate effect
- [ ] Intermediate effect logically leads to obstacle
- [ ] The "pressure to [action]" makes sense
- [ ] **WHO feels pressure is clear**
- [ ] Evidence supports the chain

**❌ Common Error:** Making subject create pressure on themselves
**✅ Correct:** Context creates pressure on subject

---

### **PHASE 6: QUESTION-SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS**

#### **6.1 Does Question Ask for Recommendations?**

**If YES:**

- [ ] Recommendations are included in Para 3 (not conclusion)
- [ ] Recommendations are SPECIFIC (not vague like "balanced policies")
- [ ] Para 3 structure: Shortened challenges (~35w) + Recommendations (~35w)
- [ ] Uses phrase: "To address these concerns, I recommend that..."
- [ ] 2-3 concrete actions stated

**If NO:**
- [ ] No recommendations needed
- [ ] Full Para 3 for challenges

#### **6.2 Does Question Ask Two Parts?**

**Example:** "How important? Why hard?"

- [ ] Para 2 answers first part
- [ ] Para 3 answers second part
- [ ] Both parts clearly addressed

---

### **PHASE 7: FORMATTING & STYLE**

**Academic prose rules:**

- [ ] **7.1 No casual/technical formatting**
  - ❌ (5-10% per day)
  - ✅ five to ten percent per day
  - ❌ 3 key measures
  - ✅ three key measures
  - ❌ e.g., etc.
  - ✅ such as, for instance, and so on

- [ ] **7.2 Numbers written out**
  - Write numbers in words (one, two, three...)
  - Write percentages in words (five percent, ten percent)

- [ ] **7.3 No parentheses for numbers/statistics**
  - Integrate into sentence naturally

- [ ] **7.4 Academic but natural tone**
  - Formal but not overly complex
  - Clear and readable
  - B1-B2 vocabulary level

---

### **PHASE 8: EVIDENCE WITHOUT FAKE CITATIONS**

- [ ] **8.1 No fake research citations**
  - ❌ "According to research by [vague experts], 80% of..."
  - ✅ "Employees who X demonstrate Y"

- [ ] **8.2 Evidence is concrete and specific**
  - Not: "many people experienced benefits"
  - Yes: "workers with clear boundaries report improved wellbeing"

- [ ] **8.3 Comparison included**
  - "...compared to those who..." present
  - Shows contrast effectively

---

### **PHASE 9: WORD COUNT**

- [ ] **9.1 Total: 230-240 words** (ideal)
  - Acceptable range: 230-250 words
  - Never below 220 or above 260

- [ ] **9.2 Section balance:**
  - Introduction: ~45 words
  - Para 2: ~75-85 words
  - Para 3: ~70-75 words (or split if recommendations)
  - Conclusion: ~35-40 words

- [ ] **9.3 Counted accurately**
  - Use word counter tool
  - Verify before submitting

---

### **PHASE 10: FINAL QUALITY CHECK**

**Read the essay out loud or carefully:**

- [ ] **10.1 Natural flow**
  - Sounds like academic writing
  - Not robotic or template-obvious
  - Transitions smooth

- [ ] **10.2 Topic paraphrasing**
  - Main topic repeated 6-7 times (not 10+)
  - 4-5 paraphrase variations used
  - Not excessive repetition

- [ ] **10.3 Consistency**
  - Position maintained throughout
  - No contradictions
  - Clear argument

- [ ] **10.4 Completeness**
  - All parts of question answered
  - Nothing missing
  - Follows template completely

## 🎯 SPECIAL CHECKS (Based on Common Errors)

### **Critical Error #1: Para 3 Sentence 3 Subject**

**This error appeared in 5 out of 6 examples!**

❌ **WRONG:**
```
"Students create pressure to..."
"Schools create pressure to balance..."
"People create pressure to spend..."
```

✅ **RIGHT:**
```
"Students experience pressure to..."
"Schools put pressure on educators to..."
"People find themselves..."
"Workplaces create pressure for employees to..."
```

**Always ask:** WHO feels the pressure? Is the subject creating pressure on themselves (illogical)?

---

### **Critical Error #2: Casual Formatting**

❌ **WRONG:**
```
(5-10% per day)
3 measures
$50
e.g., etc.
```

✅ **RIGHT:**
```
five to ten percent per day
three measures
fifty dollars
such as, for instance, and so on
```

---

### **Critical Error #3: C1 Vocabulary**

**Always check these common C1 words:**
- intensive, optimal, passively, procrastination, rigid
- subsequently, regardless, accountability
- mitigate, exacerbate, ameliorate

**Replace with B1-B2 alternatives!**

---

### **Critical Error #4: Vague Recommendations**

When question asks for recommendations:

❌ **TOO VAGUE:**
```
"implement balanced policies"
"use reasonable measures"
"create fair systems"
```

✅ **SPECIFIC:**
```
"implement moderate daily deductions of five to ten percent"
"establish documented extension procedures for emergencies"
"provide deadline reminders one week before due dates"
```

---

### **Critical Error #5: Abstract Actions**

Para 3 sentence 3 needs CONCRETE actions:

❌ **TOO ABSTRACT:**
```
"create pressure to cope with schedules"
"create pressure to deal with situations"
"create pressure to manage issues"
```

✅ **CONCRETE:**
```
"create pressure to memorize facts rather than understand concepts"
"create pressure to respond to emails after hours"
"create pressure to sacrifice sleep and personal time"
```
