## 🎯 When to Use PROBLEM-SOLUTION Template

### **Use PROBLEM-SOLUTION when question asks:**

✅ **"What is the most pressing problem... AND give solutions"**
- Two-part requirement: Identify + Solve

✅ **"Give your recommendations"**
- Explicitly asks for solutions/recommendations

✅ **"What should be done about X?"**
- Focus on actionable solutions

### **Questions Covered (2 total):**

| # | Question | Type |
|---|----------|------|
| Q10 | Most Pressing Global Problem + Solutions | Identify problem + solve |
| Q8* | Late Assignment Penalties + Recommendations | Opinion + recommendations |

*Note: Q8 can use either PRIMARY or PROBLEM-SOLUTION. Use PROBLEM-SOLUTION when you want to emphasize recommendations.

## 📝 PROBLEM-SOLUTION Template Structure

### **Word Budget:**
- Introduction: 45 words
- Para 2 (Why this problem is pressing): 70 words
- Para 3 (Solutions/Recommendations): 85 words
- Conclusion: 35 words
- **Total: ~235 words**

---

### **INTRODUCTION (45 words)**

```
The question of [problem identification] has attracted considerable 
attention in modern society. I contend that [specific problem] 
represents the most pressing challenge for [subject] despite [other 
issues], and the following analysis outlines the problem's severity 
and practical solutions.
```

**Key Points:**
- Identify the specific problem clearly
- State why it's most pressing/important
- Preview that solutions will be provided

---

### **PARAGRAPH 2 - WHY PROBLEM IS PRESSING (70 words)**

```
To begin with, one compelling reason why [problem] is most urgent 
is its potential to cause [negative outcome 1]. In particular, 
[problem] can generate [negative outcome 2], ultimately leading to 
broader consequences such as [negative outcome 3].

[Subject group] experiencing [problem] demonstrate [negative impacts]. 
For instance, [specific subjects] affected by [problem] face 
significantly [evidence of harm] compared to those [without problem].
```

**Key Points:**
- Show severity of problem
- Use concrete evidence
- Demonstrate widespread impact
- Make urgency clear

---

### **PARAGRAPH 3 - SOLUTIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS (85 words)**

```
However, effective solutions can address [problem]. This is largely 
because [solution 1] is likely to generate [positive outcome], which 
may subsequently lead to [broader benefit]. Additionally, [solution 2] 
can provide [specific benefit] through [implementation method].

Based on these considerations, I recommend that [stakeholders] should 
[specific action 1] and [specific action 2]. [Context showing 
feasibility] demonstrates that these solutions [IDIOM HERE] can 
deliver [expected results]. As a result, [stakeholders] can [achieve 
desired outcome].
```

**Key Points:**
- Provide 2-3 concrete solutions
- Show how solutions address root causes
- Make recommendations specific and actionable
- Use ONE idiom naturally here
- Demonstrate feasibility

---

### **CONCLUSION (35 words)**

```
Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that [problem] 
remains the most pressing challenge, while [solutions] offer practical 
pathways forward. Thus, it is essential for [stakeholders] to implement 
these recommendations effectively.
```

**Key Points:**
- Reaffirm problem's importance
- Reinforce that solutions are viable
- Call to action

| **Para 2** | Benefits/Support | Why X better | Why problem serious |
| **Para 3** | Challenges | Y has value | **SOLUTIONS** |

## ❌ Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why Wrong | Fix |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| **No clear problem stated** | Vague positioning | "Climate change is the most pressing" |
| **Solutions too general** | Not actionable | "Governments should invest in..." not "Something should be done" |
| **No implementation details** | Unrealistic | Show HOW solutions work |
| **Forgetting recommendations** | Incomplete answer | Para 3 must have "I recommend..." |
| **Too many solutions** | Unfocused | 2-3 concrete solutions maximum |

## Example 1: Most Pressing Global Problem

### **Question:**
> "The world's governments and organizations are facing a lot of issues. Which do you think is the most pressing problem for the inhabitants on our planet? And give solutions."

### **Question Type:** Identify Problem + Provide Solutions

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 10):**

**Problem Identified:** climate change

**Subjects:** people, nations, governments

**Why It's Pressing (Negative Effects):**
- rising temperatures and extreme weather
- environmental damage and habitat loss
- sea level rise and food shortages
- severe weather and economic harm

**Solutions:**
- immediate action and policy changes
- clean energy and carbon reduction
- international cooperation
- renewable energy investment

**Idiom:** "turn the tide" (reverse negative trends)

**Stakeholders:** governments and international organizations

---

### **Complete Essay (238 words)**

The question of **which global problem requires most urgent attention** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. I contend that **climate change** represents the most pressing challenge for **people worldwide** despite **other important issues**, and the following analysis outlines the problem's severity and practical solutions.

To begin with, one compelling reason why **climate change** is most urgent is its potential to cause **rising temperatures and extreme weather events**. In particular, **climate change** can generate **environmental damage and habitat loss**, ultimately leading to broader consequences such as **sea level rise and food shortages**. **Nations experiencing climate impacts** demonstrate **severe economic and social disruption**. For instance, **countries affected by extreme weather** face significantly **greater resource strain and population displacement** compared to those **with stable climate conditions**.

However, effective solutions can address **climate change**. This is largely because **immediate policy changes and clean energy investment** are likely to generate **reduced carbon emissions**, which may subsequently lead to **climate stabilization and environmental recovery**. Additionally, **international cooperation on climate targets** can provide **coordinated global action** through **binding agreements and shared technology**.

Based on these considerations, I recommend that **governments** should **implement aggressive renewable energy policies** and **establish binding international climate commitments**. **Countries adopting comprehensive climate strategies** demonstrate that these solutions **can turn the tide on emissions growth** and deliver **measurable environmental improvements**. As a result, **global leaders** can **achieve meaningful climate stabilization**.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that **climate change** remains the most pressing challenge, while **coordinated policy action and clean energy transition** offer practical pathways forward. Thus, it is essential for **governments and international organizations** to implement these recommendations effectively.

## Example 2: Late Assignment Penalties (with Recommendations)

### **Question:**
> "Some universities deduct students' marks if assignments are given late. What is your opinion and give your recommendations?"

### **Question Type:** Opinion + Recommendations

### **Fill-in Terms (from B1 Example 8):**

**Position:** Late penalties are beneficial BUT need flexibility

**Subjects:** students, universities

**Why Penalties Work (Benefits):**
- time management and planning skills
- personal responsibility and deadline awareness
- work discipline and professional preparation

**Problems with Strict Penalties:**
- unfair situations and student stress
- emergency circumstances
- inflexibility

**Solutions/Recommendations:**
- flexible extension policies
- clear documentation requirements
- reasonable grace periods
- emergency provisions

**Idiom:** "strike a balance" (find middle ground)

**Stakeholders:** universities and instructors

---

### **Complete Essay (236 words)**

The question of **whether universities should implement grade penalties for late submissions** has attracted considerable attention in modern society. I contend that **late assignment penalties serve important purposes** for **students** despite **requiring flexibility**, and the following analysis outlines the benefits and practical recommendations.

To begin with, one compelling reason why **late penalties** are beneficial is their potential to develop **time management and planning skills**. In particular, **deadline requirements** can generate **personal responsibility and deadline awareness**, ultimately leading to broader outcomes such as **improved work discipline and professional preparation**. **Students facing clear deadlines** demonstrate **better organizational abilities**. For instance, **learners with consistent deadline enforcement** develop significantly **stronger time management skills** compared to those **without penalty systems**.

However, effective policies must address fairness concerns. This is largely because **flexible extension procedures** are likely to generate **fair treatment of genuine emergencies**, which may subsequently lead to **reduced student stress while maintaining accountability**. Additionally, **reasonable grace periods with documentation** can provide **appropriate flexibility** through **clear emergency provisions**.

Based on these considerations, I recommend that **universities** should **implement moderate penalties** (such as 5-10% deductions per day) and **establish clear extension request procedures requiring documentation**. **Institutions using balanced policies** demonstrate that these approaches **strike a balance between accountability and compassion** and deliver **both student learning and fairness**. As a result, **universities** can **maintain academic standards while supporting students facing legitimate challenges**.

Taken together, these considerations demonstrate that **late penalties with flexibility** offer the best approach. Thus, it is essential for **universities and instructors** to implement these recommendations effectively.

## 🎯 Solution Writing Tips

### **Making Solutions Specific:**

**❌ Too General:**
```
"Governments should do something about climate change."
```

**✅ Specific and Actionable:**
```
"Governments should implement aggressive renewable energy policies 
and establish binding international climate commitments."
```

### **Structure for Solutions:**

```
WHO should do WHAT through HOW

WHO: governments, universities, companies
WHAT: implement policies, establish procedures, invest in
HOW: through legislation, binding agreements, clear guidelines
```

### **Showing Feasibility:**

```
[Evidence from reality] demonstrates that [solution] can [result].

"Countries adopting comprehensive climate strategies demonstrate 
that these solutions can turn the tide on emissions."
```
